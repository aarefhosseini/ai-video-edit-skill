# Reference research and performance

Before making a new video, establish the subject, audience, and destination. For new creative direction, search for closely related examples and inspect the user's references first. Research effort should fit the job; a narrow correction to an approved layout doesn't require rediscovering the whole genre.

Log exact URL, creator/title, inspection date, what was accessible, observable metrics with units, and the specific useful choice. Examples with modest visible metrics can still be good aesthetic references. Public likes/comments are not views, purchases, or retention; don't infer sales or virality from a famous account.

When the media is accessible, extract:

- First frame, first spoken promise, and how quickly the subject becomes clear.
- Narrative sequence: question/problem → demonstration/proof → payoff/ending.
- Actual cut times and relevant changes in scene/layout, not an invented cut frequency.
- Caption density/read time, emphasis, and whether graphics explain something.
- Audio bed, timing, pauses, transitions, and any music event aligned with a visual.
- Face/project proportions, mobile legibility, and how key facts are shown.

Translate observations into this user's choices, with rationale. Example: “Your important number is unreadable at this scale; a close view while you explain it will make the claim easier to follow.” A reference's time limit, colors, CTA, or financial story isn't a rule for every user.

If access fails, record it and use supplied media or available official/public sources. Never claim complete audiovisual inspection from a few frames. Do not evade login/access restrictions or download media to work around display restrictions.

## After publication

All phases are supported from the first release as agent workflows. Post-publication analysis uses **user-provided or explicitly authorized read-only data**. Ask for the actual post and time window, reach/views, retention if available, saves/shares/comments, and relevant outcomes. Normalize the comparison period and distinguish correlation from causal evidence. Identify drop-off points only from actual retention data. Suggest the next test and preserve its hypothesis in the project record. No publishing, account management, DMs, or fake metrics.

Aim to improve attention, clarity, relevance, and shareability. Never promise that an edit will go viral.
